General FAQs (13 questions)

Copper Sulphate is an inorganic copper salt of sulphuric acid, most commonly encountered as bright blue crystals. It is manufactured for use across agriculture, animal feed, electroplating, water treatment, laboratories and several industrial processes.

The hydrated form we supply is CuSO₄·5H₂O (Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate). The anhydrous form, with the water removed, is CuSO₄.

It is also known as Cupric Sulphate, Blue Vitriol, Bluestone, Roman Vitriol, and Copper(II) Sulphate.

In its pentahydrate form it appears as bright blue crystals or crystalline powder. Anhydrous Copper Sulphate, by contrast, is a pale grey-white powder.

The blue colour comes from the way copper(II) ions absorb light when surrounded by water molecules in the crystal lattice — a characteristic property of hydrated copper compounds. Remove the water (anhydrous form) and the blue colour disappears.

Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate (CuSO₄·5H₂O) is the standard, stable, hydrated form of Copper Sulphate that contains five water molecules per molecule of copper sulphate. It is the base material for all the grades and forms we manufacture.

Anhydrous Copper Sulphate is Copper Sulphate with its water of crystallisation removed, appearing as a pale grey-white powder. It readily reabsorbs moisture from the air and turns blue again, which is why it is sometimes used as a moisture indicator.

It is typically produced by reacting copper metal, copper oxide or copper scrap with sulphuric acid, followed by crystallisation, centrifugation, drying and grading to the required particle size and purity.

A natural mineral form, chalcanthite, exists but is rare. Nearly all commercial Copper Sulphate, including ours, is manufactured under controlled industrial conditions to ensure consistent purity.

It is a crystalline solid, blue in the hydrated form, odourless, with a bulk density of roughly 1,100–1,300 kg/m³, fully soluble in water, and non-flammable.

It is a moderately acidic salt (pH 3.5–4.5 in 1% solution), stable at room temperature, loses water of crystallisation on heating, and reacts with alkalis, carbonates and certain metals in solution.

Yes. Copper Sulphate is highly water soluble, and solubility increases further as water temperature rises.

Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate (CuSO₄·5H₂O) has a molecular weight of approximately 249.68 g/mol; the anhydrous form (CuSO₄) is approximately 159.60 g/mol.

Applications (15 questions)

Copper Sulphate is used in agriculture (fungicides, micronutrients), aquaculture, water treatment, electroplating, mining, textile and leather processing, animal feed, laboratories, and chemical manufacturing.

It controls fungal and bacterial crop diseases as part of copper-based fungicides, and corrects copper deficiency in soils and plants when used as a micronutrient.

It is applied via soil treatment, foliar spray or fertigation to supply copper — an essential trace element for plant enzyme function and chlorophyll production — in copper-deficient soils.

It is combined with lime (as in Bordeaux mixture) or other carriers to create a copper-based fungicide that is sprayed on crops to prevent fungal spore germination.

Bordeaux mixture is a fungicide made by combining Copper Sulphate with hydrated lime and water, traditionally used on grapevines and now widely applied to potatoes, tomatoes and orchard crops to prevent fungal and bacterial diseases.

In controlled, low doses it is used in fish ponds to manage algae blooms and treat certain external parasites, calculated carefully against pond volume and water hardness.

It is applied as an algaecide in reservoirs, canals and industrial cooling systems to control algae growth that affects water quality and system efficiency.

Dissolved in sulphuric acid, it forms the electrolyte solution used in copper electroplating baths, electro-refining, and printed circuit board manufacturing.

It acts as an activator in froth flotation, helping separate valuable sulphide minerals such as zinc and lead ores from waste rock during mineral processing.

It functions as a mordant that helps dyes bind more effectively and evenly to textile fibres during dyeing.

It is used in specific tanning and finishing formulations within leather processing.

It serves as a reagent for titrations, standard solution preparation, Fehling's and Benedict's solutions, and various analytical and educational experiments.

Yes — small, reagent-pack quantities of our Laboratory Grade are supplied for classroom chemistry demonstrations, most commonly crystal-growing experiments, under teacher supervision.

Yes, Feed Grade Copper Sulphate is included in poultry, swine and cattle premixes as a source of dietary trace-mineral copper.

It serves as a raw material and intermediate for producing other copper compounds and specialty chemicals across various downstream manufacturing processes.

Agriculture (9 questions)

Grapes, potatoes, tomatoes, citrus, coffee, cotton, and various orchard and plantation crops commonly use copper-based fungicides or copper micronutrient correction, particularly in copper-deficient or high-disease-pressure regions.

Symptoms include stunted growth, yellowing or die-back of young leaves, poor grain fill in cereals, and increased susceptibility to certain fungal diseases. A soil or tissue test is the reliable way to confirm deficiency before treatment.

Dosage depends on crop, soil type, deficiency severity and application method, and should always follow the product label, a soil test recommendation, or guidance from a qualified agronomist rather than a generic figure.

Yes, dilute Copper Sulphate solutions can be delivered through drip/fertigation systems, though filtration and correct dosing are important to avoid clogging and localised over-application.

Yes, a dilute solution can be foliar-sprayed to correct micronutrient deficiency or as part of a copper fungicide programme, generally applied during cooler parts of the day to reduce leaf stress.

Copper Sulphate is generally compatible with most granular NPK fertilizers when blended dry, but compatibility with specific liquid fertilizers should be tested first, as some combinations can cause precipitation.

Some tank-mix combinations are used in practice, but compatibility varies by product — always check the pesticide label and consult an agronomist before mixing, since certain combinations can reduce efficacy or cause phytotoxicity.

Copper-based fungicides, including those using Copper Sulphate, are permitted under many organic farming standards subject to usage limits — check your specific certifying body's approved-inputs list before use.

Application frequency depends on disease pressure, weather and crop stage, and should follow label instructions and local agricultural extension advice rather than a fixed schedule.

Aquaculture (6 questions)

It is used at carefully controlled doses to manage algae blooms and certain external parasites in pond aquaculture systems.

Copper ions released in solution disrupt algal cell function, killing or suppressing algae growth when applied at the correct concentration for the water body.

Dosage depends on pond volume, water alkalinity/hardness and the target organism, and should be calculated by a qualified aquaculture professional — over-dosing can harm fish.

At correctly calculated, low concentrations it is used safely in aquaculture, but fish — especially in soft, low-alkalinity water — are sensitive to copper, so dosage must be calculated carefully.

It should be avoided in low-alkalinity/soft water without dose adjustment, in ponds with sensitive or stressed fish stock, and where copper residues could affect downstream water use, unless guided by an aquaculture specialist.

Active duration varies with water chemistry, temperature and organic load, typically ranging from several days to a couple of weeks before copper binds to sediment or organic matter.

Electroplating (4 questions)

Dissolved in sulphuric acid it forms the electrolyte for copper plating baths, supplying the copper ions that deposit onto the workpiece during the plating process.

We recommend our Electroplating Grade (99%+ purity, low insoluble matter and chloride) for plating and PCB applications, as trace impurities can cause visible plating defects.

Bath concentration varies by plating process and equipment, typically formulated by the plating chemist or bath supplier to match target deposit thickness and current density.

Through periodic filtration, correct copper-to-acid ratio monitoring, contaminant control, and regular chemical analysis to keep the bath within its operating specification.

Water Treatment (4 questions)

Copper ions released into the water disrupt algal cell processes, controlling growth in reservoirs, canals and cooling water systems.

Copper Sulphate is used in some raw water bodies (reservoirs, canals) for algae control upstream of treatment, but it is not used as a direct potable/drinking water additive — treatment for drinking water should follow local health authority regulations.

Dosage depends on water volume, alkalinity and target algae species, and should be set according to relevant water treatment guidelines or a water treatment professional's recommendation.

Treatment frequency depends on regrowth rates, water conditions and seasonal algae pressure — typically assessed through periodic water quality monitoring rather than a fixed interval.

Animal Feed (4 questions)

Copper is an essential trace mineral supporting enzyme function, iron metabolism, immune response and growth performance in livestock, and Copper Sulphate is a cost-effective, bioavailable source.

Poultry, swine and cattle are the most common recipients of copper supplementation in commercial feed premixes, though requirements and tolerances vary significantly by species.

Inclusion levels vary by species, age and life stage, and should be set by a qualified animal nutritionist or veterinarian in line with applicable feed regulations rather than a single generic figure.

Yes. Copper has a narrower safety margin than many trace minerals, and species such as sheep are especially sensitive — over-supplementation can lead to copper toxicity, so accurate dosing and even distribution in the premix are essential.

Storage (4 questions)

Store in a cool, dry, well-ventilated area, away from direct sunlight, incompatible materials (strong alkalis, reducing agents, ammonia) and food or feed products, in the original sealed packaging.

When stored correctly in sealed packaging, Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate has an effectively indefinite shelf life, as it is a stable, non-degrading inorganic salt.

The pentahydrate form is relatively stable, but prolonged exposure to high humidity can cause caking or clumping — sealed, moisture-resistant packaging prevents this.

It is not recommended, as prolonged sun and rain exposure can degrade packaging and lead to caking or contamination — covered, dry storage is advised.

Technical (10 questions)

A 1% aqueous solution typically has a pH of approximately 3.5–4.5, making it mildly acidic.

Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate has a specific gravity of approximately 2.28 g/cm³; bulk density of the packaged product is typically 1,100–1,300 kg/m³.

The pentahydrate begins losing water of crystallisation around 63°C and is fully dehydrated by roughly 150°C; the anhydrous salt decomposes at higher temperatures (above 600°C) rather than melting conventionally.

Solubility increases significantly with temperature — roughly 14 g per 100 mL of water near 0°C, rising to over 75 g per 100 mL near boiling point.

Copper Sulphate Pentahydrate: CAS 7758-99-8. Anhydrous Copper Sulphate: CAS 7758-98-7.

Copper Sulphate is generally classified under HSN 2833 25 00 in the Indian customs tariff — please confirm the applicable code with your customs broker for your specific shipment.

Environmental (5 questions)

When used and disposed of according to label directions and applicable regulations, it is manageable, but it is classified as toxic to aquatic organisms and should be handled with appropriate environmental precautions.

Yes, Copper Sulphate is toxic to fish and aquatic invertebrates at elevated concentrations, which is why aquaculture and water treatment dosing must be carefully calculated and controlled.

Waste and residues should not be discharged into drains or waterways; dispose of according to local environmental regulations, typically through a licensed hazardous/chemical waste handler.

As an inorganic salt, it does not biodegrade in the way organic compounds do; copper ions persist in the environment and can accumulate in sediment, which is why controlled use and disposal matter.

Avoid over-application, prevent runoff into water bodies and drains, follow recommended dosages strictly, and dispose of containers and residues in line with local environmental regulations.
